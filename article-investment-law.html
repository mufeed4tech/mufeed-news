Cabinet Approves New Investment Law to Boost Foreign Direct Investment

YR Youssef Rashad Political Correspondent · August 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Egypt's Cabinet approved a package of amendments to the investment law this week, part of a wider push to simplify licensing and cut the time it takes new companies to start operating in priority sectors such as manufacturing, logistics, and renewable energy.

Officials say the changes consolidate approvals that previously required sign-off from multiple ministries into a single one-stop authorization — a longstanding complaint from investors weighing projects in Egypt against competing markets elsewhere in the region.

"Every extra month spent waiting on paperwork is a month a competing market is happy to take instead."

The amended law also extends tax incentives for projects located outside the Greater Cairo area, an attempt to steer new investment toward the Delta and Upper Egypt. The bill now heads to Parliament for a final vote, with officials saying they expect it to take effect before the end of the year.

Business groups have broadly welcomed the move, though some have urged the government to pair it with faster customs clearance and more predictable currency policy — two issues investors have flagged consistently in recent surveys.
